Maria

Hi — Maria from Citizens Mutual. Good to meet you. I'll write you a property policy — I need an inventory of what you have first. Here's the form. Use your list: drop each thing into the right section, and put a dollar amount on it while you're there. Most amounts come straight off a receipt; one or two you'll have to look up.

Scenario
Maria's form has four sections — Equipment, Computer Equipment, Supplies, Fixtures. Take one item into each section and put a dollar amount on it — open the receipt it came on, or look it up. Once the pattern is clear for all four, you can finish the rest in one go.

What You Discovered

✓ Every Thing on your list got a dollar amount — but the amounts didn't all come from the same place: a receipt for what you bought, an eBay comp for the gear you'd owned a while, a shelf count for the supplies.

✓ Maria has her own name for the list — insurable property: only what you own and could lose. You bought $500 of parts, but only the $200 still on the shelf goes on it.

✓ You valued one item in each section by hand, then finished the rest in one go. Same move as last time — once the pattern's clear, the volume is easy.

This is the same list of Things from last time — now sorted into categories with a number on every line. But it's only one slice of it: the part an insurer cares about. Other people will look at the very same list and see something different. The bigger picture it all belongs to is still being built — it'll have a name by the end of the module.
